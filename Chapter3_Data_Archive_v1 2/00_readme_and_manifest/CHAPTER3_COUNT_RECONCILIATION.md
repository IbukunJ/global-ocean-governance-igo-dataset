# Chapter 3 Stage 1 Literature Count Reconciliation

## Controlling screening funnel

The reconciled and controlling Stage 1 literature-screening sequence is:

**100 records identified → 90 publications assessed at full text → 79 publications included in qualitative synthesis.**

This sequence replaces the earlier working statement of **100 → 91 → 79**.

## Record-level basis

The reconciliation uses the two surviving bibliographic sheets in
`01_stage1_literature_evidence/Stage1_Literature_Evidence_ReadFirst.xlsx`.

| Evidence component | Record count | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| `1 - Literature` | 89 | Surviving candidate bibliographic export |
| `Screened Literature` | 79 | Final included corpus |
| Records present only in `Screened Literature` | 1 | Included record omitted from the candidate export |
| Unique union of both sheets | 90 | Publications assessed at full text |
| Candidate-only records | 11 | Publications excluded during eligibility/full-text review |

The screened-only publication is:

- Pattberg, Philipp; Widerberg, Oscar; (2015). *Theorising global environmental governance: Key findings and future questions*. Millennium, 43(2), 684-705.

The unique union therefore equals **89 + 1 = 90**, and the final screening decision equals
**90 − 11 = 79**.

## Why 91 is superseded

The surviving record-level evidence does not support 91 full-text publications. The earlier
91-publication figure is treated as a working-stage count that was not carried consistently into the
surviving workbook. The latest Chapter 3 screening table and flow narrative use 90 publications
assessed at full text, which is also the count recoverable from the workbook.

## Preliminary removals

The difference between 100 records identified and 90 publications assessed implies **10 preliminary
removals** through title/metadata screening and deduplication. Their bibliographic identities and
record-specific reasons were not preserved in the available files. The reconciled archive therefore
retains the aggregate count but does not invent or reconstruct unsupported record-level details.
